Ambient voice technology (AVT) and AI scribes in nursing

Published 28 November 2026 by Lincoln Gombedza, Founder of Nursing Citizen Development CIC · Educational resource (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) — not clinical guidance or a medical device.

What is ambient voice technology (AVT) in nursing? Ambient voice technology — often called an AI clinical scribe — listens to a clinical consultation with the patient's knowledge and agreement, then drafts a structured nursing note, clinic letter, or handover summary for the nurse to check, edit, and approve before anything enters the clinical record.

Across the NHS, ambient voice tools are moving rapidly from pilots into everyday outpatient, community, general practice, and ward workflows. Used safely within local governance, they can reduce screen time and let nurses stay focused on the person in front of them. Used without clinical scrutiny or formal Trust approval, they introduce serious patient safety, confidentiality, and health equity risks.

What nurses must check before using any ambient voice tool

Before switching on any AI scribe in an NHS Trust, GP practice, care home, or independent provider, confirm four governance foundations:

  • Formal organisational approval: Only use tools formally approved by your Trust or employer. Never record patient conversations on personal devices or paste transcripts into unapproved consumer AI apps.
  • Clinical safety assurance (DCB0129 & DCB0160): Confirm the supplier holds a DCB0129 clinical safety case and your deploying organisation has completed local DCB0160 clinical risk management signed off by a Clinical Safety Officer (CSO).
  • Information governance & UK GDPR: Confirm a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) is in place covering audio retention, UK/EEA data residency, and zero training of third-party models on patient audio.
  • Informed, accessible patient consent: Explain plainly that an approved tool is helping draft the notes, offer an easy opt-out with zero impact on care, and adapt explanations for patients with learning disabilities, dementia, sensory impairments, or language needs.

Safe nursing boundaries for ambient voice scribes

Area Where approved AVT can help Nursing red lines (NMC Code)
Documentation Drafting structured consultation notes, SBAR summaries, or clinic letters for review. Never auto-save or sign off an AI-drafted note without reading every line against what actually occurred.
Clinical judgement Reducing typing burden so you can observe non-verbal cues and build rapport. Never delegate triage, risk scoring, safeguarding decisions, or prescribing judgement to an AI scribe.
Language & equity Drafting plain-English follow-up summaries that you verify against Trust leaflets. Check closely for transcription bias across regional UK accents, dialects, multilingual consultations, and speech differences; never replace qualified NHS interpreters.
Safeguarding & sensitive disclosures Routine consultations where recording is appropriate and agreed. Pause ambient recording during sensitive safeguarding, domestic abuse, or mental health disclosures where recording could inhibit honesty or create risk.

The 5-point nursing check before signing an AI-drafted note

Ambient scribes can introduce subtle errors — omitting a critical negative finding, inventing a normal observation that was never checked, or softening an urgent escalation. Before signing off any AI-assisted record, check:

  1. Hallucinated negatives or observations: Did the scribe add "chest clear" or "no suicidal ideation" when those were not actually assessed?
  2. Medication names, doses and allergies: Verify every drug name, route, unit, and allergy status word-for-word.
  3. Safeguarding & capacity accuracy: Confirm exact quotes, consent status, and mental capacity assessments are faithfully recorded without smoothing over concern.
  4. Person-centred, non-stigmatising language: Replace judgemental or biased phrasing with respectful, factual nursing language.
  5. Actionable safety-netting & escalation: Confirm follow-up plans, red-flag advice, and named responsibilities are clear and accurate.

Frequently asked questions

Can NHS nurses use ambient voice scribes?

Yes, only where the tool is formally approved by your NHS Trust or employer with completed DPIA, DCB0129/DCB0160 clinical safety sign-off, and local policy. Unapproved consumer apps or personal phones must never be used to record patient consultations.

Who is accountable under the NMC Code if an AI scribe makes an error?

The registered nurse, midwife, or nursing associate who reviews and signs the record remains 100% accountable under the NMC Code. An AI scribe drafts text; it does not hold an NMC PIN or share regulatory accountability.

Can student nurses use AI scribes on placement?

Student nurses should only use Trust-approved ambient voice tools under the direct guidance of their practice supervisor or assessor, following both host Trust and university AI policies. Practising manual clinical documentation remains essential for building clinical reasoning. See our dedicated toolkit page: AI scribes on placement.

What about MHRA medical device rules?

Basic transcription and summarisation tools sit differently from systems that diagnose, triage, or recommend treatment — which may be regulated as Software as a Medical Device (SaMD) by the MHRA. Always check the supplier's intended purpose statement and local Clinical Safety Officer guidance.
