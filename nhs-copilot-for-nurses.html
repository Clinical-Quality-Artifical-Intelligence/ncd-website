NHS Digital & Microsoft 365 Copilot

Microsoft 365 Copilot for NHS nurses: safe prompts, licences and governance

Published 28 November 2026 by Lincoln Gombedza, Founder of Nursing Citizen Development CIC · Independent educational resource (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) — not affiliated with or endorsed by Microsoft or NHS England.

How can NHS nurses use Microsoft 365 Copilot safely? Microsoft 365 Copilot and Copilot Chat are now appearing on NHS desktops across the UK. Used within local Trust governance and with zero patient-identifiable data, Copilot helps nurses save hours on policy summaries, ward huddles, teaching prep, quality improvement, and CPD — while keeping every clinical decision 100% with the nurse.

Copilot Chat vs full Microsoft 365 Copilot in the NHS

Before running a prompt, check which version your NHS Trust provides:

  • Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat: Web-grounded chat signed in with your NHS work account (look for the green enterprise data protection shield). Works great for drafting, summarising public policies, teaching prep, and structuring anonymised reflections.
  • Full Microsoft 365 Copilot licence: Adds Copilot directly inside Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook, and Teams meetings (e.g., summarising team meetings or organising non-identifiable audit spreadsheets).

The NCD Nursing Prompt Cycle (Assess, Plan, Implement, Evaluate)

Instead of memorising tech jargon, structure every nursing prompt using the nursing process you already know:

  1. Assess: State the safe, non-identifiable context, audience, and NHS/UK setting.
  2. Plan: Specify the format, length, reading level, and clear boundaries (e.g. UK spelling, plain English, no invented citations).
  3. Implement: Run the prompt in your approved Copilot workspace.
  4. Evaluate: Check every clinical fact, policy reference, and tone against BNF, NICE, and Trust guidance before using it.

3 safe starter prompts for NHS nurses (zero patient data)

  • Policy to 2-minute ward safety huddle: "Act as a UK clinical practice educator. Summarise the key nursing actions from this public NHS guideline into a 2-minute ward safety huddle script with 3 takeaway bullet points and 1 reflection question for staff."
  • Teaching & OSCE revision quiz: "Act as a UK nursing tutor. Create 5 multiple-choice revision questions with brief rationales on [clinical topic, e.g. sepsis recognition / NEWS2] aligned to UK practice. Remind me to verify exact thresholds against current NICE guidance."
  • Quality improvement (QI) starter plan: "Draft a simple Plan-Do-Study-Act (PDSA) template for a ward team aiming to improve [general non-identifiable process, e.g. timely discharge summaries], listing measurable process metrics and staff engagement steps."
